# App name brainstorm

## What the name should convey

The app is a fast, friendly strength-training log for people who may work out alone, as a pair, or with a family or crew. A strong name should ideally feel:

- Energetic without taking itself too seriously.
- Welcoming to solo lifters, partners, families, and friends.
- Easy to say, spell, remember, and recognize on a phone.
- Connected to lifting, progress, or training together.
- Distinct enough to search for and protect as a brand.

The name does not need to explain every feature. The icon, subtitle, and App Store description can make the workout-logging purpose explicit.

## Current direction: LiftChase

As of August 28, 2026, **LiftChase** is the preferred working name. It follows the Chase-based portfolio strategy documented in the user-level app naming guidance: a one-word CamelCase compound with `Chase` as the suffix and a first word that represents something the customer genuinely pursues.

**LiftChase** is short, easy to say and spell, clearly related to strength training, and more distinctive than the crowded `Gym`, `Workout`, `Tracker`, and `Log` naming patterns. The shared-workout differentiator can be expressed in the App Store subtitle instead of forcing the entire feature set into the name.

- **Proposed App Store name:** LiftChase
- **Proposed subtitle:** *Shared strength workout log*
- **Spoken positioning:** “LiftChase keeps every lifter's sets, weights, and progress together.”
- **Temporary local display name:** Lift Chase

The temporary display name intentionally uses a space so it reads naturally beneath the app icon during local testing. It does not rename the Xcode project, target, scheme, product, bundle identifier, or repository.

## App Store naming survey

On August 28, 2026, four broad searches of Apple's public U.S. App Store catalog returned 540 unique relevant titles. The most common naming words were:

| Word | Titles containing the word |
| --- | ---: |
| Workout | 284 |
| Tracker | 231 |
| Gym | 182 |
| Log | 139 |
| Strength | 55 |
| Fitness | 53 |
| Training | 45 |
| Lift | 14 |

The recognizable products generally use one of three approaches:

- Standalone brands such as **Hevy**, **Strong**, **Fitbod**, and **JEFIT**.
- Fitness compounds such as **StrongLifts**, **StrengthLog**, **RepCount**, **HeavySet**, and **Liftin'**.
- Generic keyword stacks such as “Gym Workout Tracker Log,” which may perform a descriptive search function but are difficult to remember or defend as brands.

This supports using a compact product name such as **LiftChase** and putting category keywords in the subtitle.

## Naming directions

### Togetherness and partnership

This direction best captures the app's unusual ability to track several people's progress in one workout.

- **Spotter** — immediately connected to lifting and suggests trusted support.
- **Tandem** — polished, warm, and clearly about doing something together, though not obviously a fitness app on its own.
- **Super Set** — combines familiar gym language with the idea of a group or set of people.
- **Setmates** — friendly and specific to working out together.
- **Rep Crew** — energetic, inclusive, and descriptive.
- **Lift Crew** — simple and welcoming, with a clear group-training angle.
- **Rack Pack** — playful and memorable; leans more toward gym culture.
- **Training Partners** — direct and inclusive, but less distinctive.
- **Together Strong** — supportive and accessible, though more like a slogan than a product name.
- **Side by Side** — emphasizes companionship but needs a fitness-oriented subtitle.

### Progress and accomplishment

This direction emphasizes consistency, personal records, and getting stronger over time.

- **PR** — compact and recognizable to lifters, but extremely broad and difficult to search for.
- **Next Rep** — forward-looking and easy to understand.
- **One More** — motivational and memorable without sounding aggressive.
- **Up a Set** — playful reference to both progression and workout structure.
- **Rep by Rep** — communicates steady progress and habit building.
- **Personal Best** — positive and accessible, but generic.
- **Mighty** — warm, optimistic, and broad enough to grow beyond lifting.
- **Strong Today** — friendly and focused on showing up rather than competing.
- **Stacked** — evokes plates and progress, with a confident tone.
- **Level Up** — clear progression language, though common across many categories.

### Gym culture and humor

This direction is the most expressive and memorable, but some options narrow the audience or may age quickly.

- **Pump** — short and energetic, but broad and crowded.
- **Swol** — playful internet-gym language; deliberately informal.
- **Gym Rat** — familiar and affectionate to enthusiasts, but may feel uninviting to beginners.
- **Gym Bros** — playful and social, but could make women, families, and solo users feel outside the brand.
- **I Pick Things Up** — a more usable version of “I pick things up and put them down,” though still long for an app name.
- **Pick Things Up** — lighthearted, approachable, and less macho.
- **Lift Happens** — memorable and humorous, but novelty-forward.
- **Heavy Friends** — emphasizes companionship, though “heavy” has mixed connotations.
- **Swolemates** — strong partner-workout pun, but may imply a romantic pair rather than any crew.
- **Reps & Company** — playful and inclusive with room for a polished visual identity.

### Characters, icons, and personalities

These names have instant personality, but names tied to real people or established fictional characters would need careful trademark, publicity-rights, and App Store research before use.

- **Arnold** — iconic strength association and a friendly human name; strongly tied to Arnold Schwarzenegger.
- **Hans** — evokes a broad strongman character without explaining the product.
- **Popeye** — instantly associated with strength, but inseparable from an existing character and brand.
- **Gunny** — compact drill-sergeant energy without using a specific person's name.
- **Sarge** — motivational and recognizable, though potentially harsh for a friendly family app.
- **Major Gains** — a gym pun with drill-sergeant flavor.
- **Hartman** — a recognizable fictional drill-sergeant reference with substantial rights and tone concerns.
- **Reveille** — distinctive military-training language, best suited to a disciplined early-morning concept.

### Habit and routine

This direction is approachable and supports consistency, although it says less about the app's shared-workout advantage.

- **Early Bird** — upbeat and habit-oriented, but wrongly implies that workouts happen in the morning.
- **On the Regular** — casual consistency language.
- **Show Up** — encouraging and beginner-friendly.
- **The Routine** — directly connected to the app's core organizing object, but generic.
- **Daily Strength** — clear and positive, though workouts need not be daily.
- **Rep Ritual** — memorable and habit-oriented.
- **Good Form** — works for both consistency and exercise technique.

## Parent-and-child direction

These names focus specifically on parents modeling healthy habits, children growing into strength, and families progressing together. **Raised Strong** is the current favorite in this direction.

| Name | Why it works | Suggested subtitle | Main concern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Set by Example** | A strong double meaning: completing workout sets and teaching children through action. | *Strong habits start together.* | The fitness meaning may not be obvious without the subtitle or icon. |
| **Kin & Sets** | Compact, warm, and directly connects family with strength training. | *Family strength, one set at a time.* | “Kin” may feel slightly old-fashioned to some people. |
| **Raised Strong** | Connects raising children with developing physical and emotional strength. | *Grow strong together.* | Could sound like a parenting program rather than a workout log. |
| **Like Father, Like Lift** | A playful father-and-son variation on a familiar phrase that makes the relationship and lifting focus explicit. | *Strong habits, handed down.* | Deliberately excludes mothers, daughters, and other training relationships. |
| **Next Gen Sets** | Clearly evokes one generation introducing the next to lifting. | *Strength for every generation.* | Has a slightly more technical or program-like tone. |
| **Home Team Lifts** | Frames the family as a supportive team without limiting the app to young children. | *Train together. Grow together.* | The name is longer and may imply workouts happen only at home. |

No exact matches for these six names appeared in the public U.S. App Store Health & Fitness results during the August 27, 2026 screen. **Roots & Reps** and **Mighty Roots** were also considered, but existing fitness-event and broader brand usage made them weaker candidates.

## Before choosing

- Test each finalist aloud in phrases such as “Open ___,” “I logged it in ___,” and “Download ___.”
- Check the App Store, major search engines, domains, and social handles for confusingly similar fitness products.
- Run a proper trademark screen before committing to artwork or a public release.
- Mock the finalists under the existing app icon and on the welcome screen.
- Ask a mix of solo lifters, couples, parents, beginners, and experienced gym users what each name implies before explaining the app.
